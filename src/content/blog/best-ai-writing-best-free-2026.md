Not every writer needs a £50-a-month subscription. But not every "free" AI writing tool is actually useful once you hit the word cap three days into the month. This guide cuts through the noise: here are the AI writing tools with genuine free plans, what each one actually gives you, and exactly what you sacrifice by staying on the free tier.

## The Free Plans at a Glance: A Quick Checklist

Before diving deep, run through this checklist to know what you need:

- **Volume:** How many words do you realistically write per month?
- **Use case:** Blog posts, social captions, emails, or everything?
- **Languages:** Do you write in anything other than English?
- **SEO:** Do you need built-in SEO features, or do you handle that separately?
- **Team size:** Just you, or do others need access?

Keep your answers in mind as you read on.

## Tool-by-Tool Breakdown: Free Plans Compared

### Rytr — Best Free Plan Overall

If you only remember one name from this article, make it [Rytr](/tools/rytr). Its free tier gives you **10,000 characters per month** — by far the most generous limit here — along with 40+ use cases and multiple tone options. For students, solo bloggers, or anyone dipping their toes into AI writing, that's a meaningful amount of content.

**What you give up on the free plan:**
- Access to 35+ languages (locked to Premium at £24.16/mo)
- Plagiarism checker
- Custom use cases
- No team features

The upgrade to Unlimited at just **$7.50/mo** is one of the most affordable jumps in the category if you outgrow the free tier. The trade-off is that Rytr is less sophisticated than enterprise tools, and brand voice training is limited. But for the price? Remarkable.

**Best for:** Students, solo creators, budget-conscious writers who need volume.

---

### Copy.ai — Free Plan for Workflow Explorers

[Copy.ai](/tools/copy-ai) positions itself as a go-to-market AI platform, and even on the free plan you get access to **90+ writing tools** and a chat interface. That breadth is impressive.

**What you give up on the free plan:**
- Only **2,000 words per month** — this runs out fast
- No brand voice
- Workflow automation is locked to paid tiers
- No SEO-specific features on any plan

The 2,000-word cap is the real sticking point. It's enough to evaluate the tool and write a handful of social posts, but not enough for regular content production. If you're a freelancer or social media manager who wants to test the platform before committing $36/mo, the free plan serves that purpose well.

**Best for:** Freelancers and small teams who want to trial the platform before upgrading.

---

### HIX.AI — Most Tools on a Free Plan

[HIX.AI](/tools/hix-ai) offers an eye-catching **120+ AI writing tools** even on its free tier, including an email writer and grammar checker. The catch: you're limited to **1,000 words per week** (roughly 4,000/month), and GPT-4 access requires the Pro plan at $49.99/mo.

**What you give up on the free plan:**
- Tight weekly word limit
- GPT-4 model (free tier uses GPT-3.5)
- Priority support
- Full long-form editor functionality

HIX.AI is a newer brand, so it lacks the track record of some rivals. That said, if you're a student or occasional content creator who wants access to a wide toolkit without paying, the free plan covers the basics. Just be aware that the AI detection bypass feature the platform markets is ethically questionable — use it carefully.

**Best for:** Budget users and students who want GPT-4 access eventually but want to start free.

---

### Writesonic — Free Plan With SEO Ambitions

[Writesonic](/tools/writesonic) markets itself as an AI search visibility platform, and it does offer a free plan. However, the specifics of that free tier's word limit aren't prominently detailed — check the official pricing page directly before signing up.

What's clear is that Writesonic's paid Individual plan ($39/mo) includes Chatsonic, an Article Writer 6.0, brand voice, and SEO tools — making it a strong option for bloggers and SEO content writers once you're ready to pay. The UI can feel overwhelming at first, and quality varies by template.

**Best for:** SEO-focused writers who want to trial the platform and scale into a paid plan.

## What You're Always Giving Up on Free Plans

Across all these tools, free plans consistently drop these features:

- **Brand voice training** — paid only, almost universally
- **Team seats** — free plans are strictly single-user
- **Priority or any meaningful support**
- **Advanced AI models** (GPT-4, etc.)
- **Workflow automation**
- **Plagiarism checking**

If any of those are non-negotiable for your workflow, budget for at least an entry-level paid plan.
