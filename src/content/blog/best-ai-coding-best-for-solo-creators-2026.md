If you're building on your own — a freelancer, indie hacker, or solo founder — you don't need an enterprise AI coding suite with SSO and annual contracts. You need something that gets out of your way, works with your existing setup, and ideally doesn't cost a fortune. The good news: the [AI coding assistant](/categories/ai-coding) space has matured enough that free plans are genuinely useful, not just bait.

Here's an honest look at what's available and who each tool actually suits.

## The Free Tier Showdown: Copilot vs Cursor vs Replit AI

Three tools in this category offer free plans, and they serve meaningfully different types of solo creator.

**[GitHub Copilot](/tools/github-copilot)** is the most widely known. On its free plan you get 2,000 completions per month and limited chat, but — and this matters — it's currently limited to VS Code. If you live in VS Code and already have your GitHub workflow locked in, this is a sensible starting point. The 2,000 completion cap is reasonably generous for part-time projects, though if you're shipping code daily you may bump into it by the third week of the month.

**[Cursor](/tools/cursor)** takes a different approach. Rather than plugging into your existing editor, it is a standalone AI-first code editor built around AI-native workflows from the ground up. The free plan offers limited completions and basic chat, plus community support rather than any formal help line. For a solo creator who is comfortable experimenting and wants AI woven into every part of the editing experience, Cursor's approach feels more cohesive. The trade-off is that you're adopting a whole new environment, which isn't trivial if you've spent years configuring your current setup.

**[Replit AI](/tools/replit-ai)** is the odd one out — and intentionally so. It runs entirely in the browser, which means zero local setup. If you're a non-technical founder prototyping an idea, or someone who codes occasionally rather than professionally, Replit removes a significant barrier. The free plan includes basic AI features, though compute is limited and repos are public. For anything sensitive or production-ready, those constraints matter.

**Bottom line on free plans:** [Replit AI](/tools/replit-ai) for beginners and non-technical founders; [Cursor](/tools/cursor) for solo devs who want an AI-native editor; [GitHub Copilot](/tools/github-copilot) for those already embedded in the VS Code and GitHub ecosystem.

## When It's Worth Paying: Tabnine vs Cody

Of the two paid options here, only one makes practical sense for solo creators.

**[Tabnine](/tools/tabnine)** starts from $12/month (check the official site for current pricing and regional rates) and works across multiple IDEs — a significant advantage over the free tools if you switch between environments. It also positions itself firmly around data privacy, which matters to freelancers handling client code. You're not feeding a client's proprietary logic into a shared model. If you work on contracts where confidentiality is a concern, Tabnine's per-user model gives you enterprise-grade data controls without needing an enterprise team. For a deeper look at how it stacks up, see our [Tabnine review](/tools/tabnine).

**[Cody by Sourcegraph](/tools/cody)** starts from $59/month per user on an annual contract. It's built for large teams with massive, multi-repo codebases — full codebase indexing, SSO, the works. These are genuinely powerful features, but they're solving problems a solo creator almost certainly doesn't have. At that price point and contract structure, it's simply not the right fit for an individual. Look at it again if your freelance practice grows into a small agency. You can read more in our [Cody review](/tools/cody).

## Practical Recommendations by Situation

- **Just starting out or non-technical:** [Replit AI](/tools/replit-ai) — browser-based, no setup, free to start.
- **Professional developer, VS Code user:** [GitHub Copilot](/tools/github-copilot) — familiar, capable free tier, integrates with your existing workflow.
- **Solo dev wanting an AI-first experience:** [Cursor](/tools/cursor) — worth the learning curve if you're all-in on AI-assisted coding.
- **Freelancer handling confidential client code:** [Tabnine](/tools/tabnine) — the privacy controls justify the monthly cost.
- **Enterprise-scale solo operator:** Keep an eye on [Cody](/tools/cody) for the future, but it's overkill right now.

Not sure which category fits your needs? Browse all options on the [AI coding tools](/categories/ai-coding) category page for a broader view.

## A Note on Free Plan Longevity

Free tiers in AI tooling are competitive bets, not permanent fixtures. Caps, features, and pricing can shift. Before committing to any workflow built around a free plan, check the current terms on the official site — what's available today may look different in six months.

The solo creator market is genuinely well-served right now. You don't need to spend much, if anything, to get meaningful AI coding assistance. Start free, validate whether it improves your output, then pay up only if and when the limitations actually bite. The right AI coding assistant won't just save you time — it'll let you build things you couldn't have tackled alone, which is the whole point of going solo in the first place.
